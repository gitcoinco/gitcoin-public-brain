# Proposal to adjust Code of Conduct WRT Airdrops

<https://gov.gitcoin.co/t/proposal-to-adjust-code-of-conduct-wrt-airdrops/8383>
owocki | 2021-08-26 15:05:51 UTC | #1

Hi everyone,

## proposal

I'd like to propose adding the following section to the [Gitcoin Code of Conduct](https://support.gitcoin.co/kb/article/42-code-of-conduct/).

> *Airdrops may come, but you are not entitled to an Airdrop.*
> Over the course of time, Gitcoin Grants has become a significant pillar of the ecosystem - in which more & more projects are reliant for support.  Because Gitcoin Grants has had 1mm+ lifetime crowdfund contributions, it has become a source of [data](https://github.com/gitcoinco/web/blob/master/docs/API.md) about which projects the community values, and who in the ecosystem is supporting these projects.
>  Any Airdrops that come to Gitcoin contributors have been on a "don't expect it, but be thankful if it comes" social norm.     
> We expect that community contributors are informed about, and participating in, the process through which governance passes proposals.   We expect that community members are graceful in accepting airdrops, but are not acting entitled to them unless a governance proposal passes that specifically entitles them to an airdrop at a specific time. Any disrespectful, harassing, entitled, or trolling behaviour related to airdrops is against the code of conduct.  

## motivation 1

The motviation for this proposal is based partially out of [this post](https://vitalik.ca/general/2021/04/02/round9.html) from Vitalik (the architect of Quadratic Funding), in which he outlines the following spectrum through which what types of motivations for contributing to grants are within the design target for QF instances:

 ![motivations|690x271](upload://mZr3xtcO0JgXTxSFA56UWpjD2Ae.png)

## motivation 2

The second motivation for this proposal comes from my experience in interacting in the public channels of the Gitcoin Discord, in which several members have confessed to me that they are there to receive airdrops.  And would not otherwise contribute to Gitcoin Grants.

In some cases, these conversations have become combative.  Conversations have become heated when contributors feel entitled to an airdrop on their timetable, without going through [the ratified Governance process](https://gov.gitcoin.co/t/gitcoin-dao-governance-process-v1/7860) and the Gitcoin Stewards or Gitcoin team cannot deliver.

By formalizing this policy in the CoC will make it clear to these contributors that their efforts to contribute only to receive airdrops are not worth their time,

tjayrush | 2021-08-27 19:15:58 UTC | #27

Thank you so much for your kind response.

What I mean by damage is this. The original purpose of the Quadratic Funding idea was that the amount of matching funds given to a grant are proportional to the number of donations they receive (as opposed to matching based on the total dollars of donations they received). This is because QF is attempting to gauge true community sentiment for a project as a way to guide matching donors in their decision on who to match and how much. This works if the measure of 'true community sentiment' is accurate. When some projects (that is some grant recipients) promise something in return for donations, any project that doesn't promise equally valuable returns will obviously suffer in the measurement of their popularity. This defeats the original intent of QF. For grants that have nothing to 'airdrop', they simply can't compete for attention, and what you end up with is not a matching system that rewards projects that of the most use to the community, but projects who pay their donors the highest reward.

Does that explain what I mean?

The term airdrop in crypto by now has the implication of getting 'something for nothing'. Some tokens are sent unannounced to existing accounts, or you click through some form just in time, or having participated with the issuer's systems in the past, you can claim some.

But no further actions or contributions are expected, hence the broad attraction, luring hoards of crypto noobs, hunting for any drops.  It's a reasonable behavior in their situation and does not make them bad. They are simply not a part of the core OSS "community", and unlikely to become such.

Is there any way to shed all use of the term 'airdrop' and stick to 'grant', which is broadly understood and not so encumbered?

And generally to stay away from promotional techniques that are so actively used by the numerous sketchy projects out there.  There is already precedent of a token stealing scam, using a giveaway https://www.theblockcrypto.com/post/112339/creative-attacker-steals-76000-in-rune-by-giving-out-free-tokens
